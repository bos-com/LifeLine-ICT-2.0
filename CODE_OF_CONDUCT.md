# Code of Conduct



## LifeLine-ICT-2.0



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 is an open-source AI, IoT and GIS platform for environmental disaster intelligence, including flood and other environmental hazard prediction, early warning, and resilient decision support in data-scarce environments.



The project is developed under the **Bugema Open Source Community (BOSC)** and welcomes contributions from students, academic staff, researchers, developers, environmental scientists, GIS specialists, IoT developers, disaster-management practitioners, and other members of the open-source community.



This Code of Conduct establishes expectations for respectful, professional, inclusive, and responsible participation in the project.



---



## 1. Our Commitment



We are committed to providing a collaborative environment where contributors can participate constructively regardless of their:



* Academic background

* Technical experience

* Professional role

* Geographic location

* Institution or organization

* Contribution size

* Programming language or technical specialization

* Level of familiarity with open-source development



Contributors are expected to treat one another with professionalism, respect, fairness, and consideration.



---



## 2. Expected Behaviour



Participants are expected to:



* Communicate respectfully and professionally.

* Welcome constructive feedback.

* Respect different technical perspectives.

* Ask questions when requirements or technical decisions are unclear.

* Give credit to contributors and original sources.

* Document important technical and research decisions.

* Provide evidence when making technical, scientific, or research claims.

* Respect project architecture and established development processes.

* Protect confidential, sensitive, or personal information.

* Follow responsible data-management practices.

* Respect software licenses and intellectual property.

* Report security vulnerabilities responsibly.

* Avoid personal attacks and hostile communication.

* Help new contributors understand the project.

* Focus discussions on the work rather than individuals.



---



## 3. Unacceptable Behaviour



The following behaviours are not acceptable:



* Harassment, intimidation, threats, or discrimination.

* Personal attacks or abusive language.

* Deliberate disruption of project activities.

* Publishing another person's private or confidential information without permission.

* Plagiarism or deliberate misrepresentation of another person's work.

* Deliberate submission of malicious code.

* Deliberate introduction of vulnerabilities.

* Falsification or manipulation of research results.

* Misrepresentation of experimental findings.

* Misuse of project infrastructure, data, credentials, or resources.

* Unauthorised access to systems or accounts.

* Repeatedly ignoring established contribution and review processes.

* Any behaviour that violates applicable laws or institutional policies.



---



## 4. Technical Discussions



Technical disagreements are expected in an open-source project.



Contributors should:



1. Clearly explain the technical problem.

2. Provide evidence, tests, documentation, or examples where appropriate.

3. Distinguish facts from assumptions.

4. Consider alternative solutions.

5. Respect decisions made through the project's established review process.

6. Escalate unresolved architectural disagreements to maintainers when necessary.



Disagreement about code, architecture, data, models, or methodology should never become a personal conflict.



---



## 5. Research and Scientific Integrity



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 includes research-oriented components and may be used in academic work.



Contributors must therefore:



* Report experimental results honestly.

* Avoid fabricating or manipulating data.

* Clearly identify simulated, synthetic, estimated, or real-world data.

* Document experimental assumptions.

* Preserve appropriate data provenance.

* Cite external datasets, models, algorithms, libraries, and publications.

* Clearly distinguish research prototypes from validated operational functionality.

* Avoid presenting unvalidated results as operational predictions.

* Respect applicable research ethics requirements.

* Follow institutional and project requirements for responsible research.



Where uncertainty exists, contributors should document it rather than conceal it.



---



## 6. Environmental and Disaster Intelligence Responsibility



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 may support environmental monitoring, hazard prediction, early warning, and decision support.



Contributors must recognize that incorrect information may have real-world consequences.



Accordingly:



* Models should be tested before being presented as operational.

* Limitations should be documented.

* Uncertainty should be communicated where appropriate.

* Data-quality problems should not be hidden.

* Sensor failures should be reported.

* Model performance should be evaluated using appropriate metrics.

* Alerts should not be represented as guaranteed predictions.

* Experimental systems should clearly indicate their maturity and limitations.



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 is intended to support human decision-making and should not be represented as replacing responsible disaster-management authorities or professional judgement.



---



## 7. Data Responsibility



Contributors working with environmental, IoT, GIS, community, or research data should:



* Respect data ownership and licensing.

* Avoid exposing personally identifiable information.

* Avoid committing sensitive credentials or secrets to the repository.

* Document dataset sources and provenance.

* Follow applicable ethical and institutional requirements.

* Use appropriate anonymisation or aggregation where necessary.

* Clearly identify sample, simulated, and production datasets.



Sensitive information must not be committed to the public repository.



---



## 8. Artificial Intelligence Responsibility



Contributors developing AI or machine-learning components should:



* Document the purpose of models.

* Document training and evaluation data where possible.

* Report relevant evaluation metrics.

* Document important assumptions and limitations.

* Avoid unsupported claims about model accuracy or reliability.

* Consider data quality and representativeness.

* Document significant preprocessing and feature-engineering steps.

* Record model versions where appropriate.

* Make experiments reproducible where reasonably possible.



AI-generated code or content should be reviewed and tested by a human contributor before being incorporated into the project.



---



## 9. Student Contributors



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 welcomes student participation.



Students are encouraged to:



* Start with clearly defined GitHub issues.

* Ask questions when requirements are unclear.

* Learn the project's Git and GitHub workflow.

* Submit small, reviewable contributions.

* Document their work.

* Participate in code reviews.

* Learn from feedback.

* Respect academic integrity requirements.



Student contributions should be evaluated based on the work submitted and documented rather than academic status alone.



---



## 10. Maintainers



Project maintainers are responsible for helping maintain a healthy development environment.



Maintainers should:



* Apply contribution rules consistently.

* Review contributions fairly.

* Explain significant technical decisions.

* Encourage constructive participation.

* Protect project infrastructure.

* Respond appropriately to reported conduct concerns.

* Protect contributors from harassment or abuse.

* Escalate serious matters when necessary.



Maintainers are also expected to follow this Code of Conduct.



---



## 11. Reporting Problems



If you experience or observe unacceptable behaviour, report it to the project maintainers.



Reports should include, where appropriate:



* A description of the incident.

* Relevant dates or times.

* The project location where the incident occurred.

* Links to relevant issues, pull requests, commits, or discussions.

* Any supporting information that can help establish what happened.



Reports should be handled respectfully and as confidentially as reasonably possible.



For security vulnerabilities, contributors should follow the procedures described in `SECURITY.md` rather than publicly posting sensitive vulnerability details.



---



## 12. Enforcement



Project maintainers may take appropriate action in response to behaviour that violates this Code of Conduct.



Possible actions may include:



* Requesting clarification or correction.

* Issuing a warning.

* Removing inappropriate content.

* Temporarily restricting participation.

* Closing issues or discussions that violate project standards.

* Removing project access where necessary.

* Escalating serious matters to the appropriate institutional or legal authorities.



Actions should be proportionate to the circumstances and consistent with project governance.



---



## 13. Scope



This Code of Conduct applies to project-related activities, including:



* GitHub repositories.

* Issues.

* Pull requests.

* Discussions.

* Code reviews.

* Documentation contributions.

* Project meetings.

* Workshops and training sessions.

* Project demonstrations.

* Other official LifeLine-ICT-2.0 activities.



---



## 14. Relationship to Other Policies



This Code of Conduct should be read together with:



* `CONTRIBUTING.md` — contribution and development procedures.

* `SECURITY.md` — security vulnerability reporting and responsible disclosure.

* `SUPPORT.md` — obtaining technical and project support.

* Project licensing terms.

* Applicable Bugema University policies.

* Applicable laws and regulations.



Where institutional or legal requirements apply, contributors are expected to comply with them.



---



## 15. Final Principle



LifeLine-ICT-2.0 is intended to be a collaborative open-source project built through shared knowledge, responsible engineering, scientific integrity, and constructive participation.



**Build responsibly. Communicate respectfully. Document your work. Test your contributions. Help others learn.**

**Maintained by the Bugema Open Source Community (BOSC).**
